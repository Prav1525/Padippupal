## Seven teaching beats

### 1. Curiosity helps us notice and ask questions
**Source chunks:** `85e8fe7b-ba04-45e3-8e88-54770f76a11b`, `540d5b48-97fc-4a93-8251-1b3e177b4b61` · **Page:** 1

**English tutor text:**  
Science begins when we notice something around us and wonder how or why it happens. A flower opening and stars shining are examples of things we can ask questions about. Curiosity does not mean we already know the answer. It gives us a question to explore.

**Tanglish tutor text:**  
Namma sutthi irukkura vishayatha gavanichu, “Idhu eppadi nadakkudhu? Yen ippadi irukku?” nu yosikkiradhudhaan curiosity. Poo eppadi malarudhu, stars yen shine aagudhu nu kelvi kekkalaam. Kelvi kekkuradhu answer theriyum-nu artham illa; adhu explore panna oru starting point.

**Inline drawing:** A flower and a night sky; a child’s observation bubble leads to a large question mark. No invented scientific answer.
**Narration beat:** Show the flower/sky sketch while explaining the examples; reveal the question mark at “wonder why”.
**Quality check:** Visual says “ask”, not “answer already known”.

### 2. Science is an ever-growing jigsaw puzzle
**Source chunks:** `c87f3be1-35c5-4b1e-b769-2e53850f6de5`, `51c5eeae-dcb2-4c04-a2ef-90e7fe5f6fc0` · **Pages:** 2–3

**English tutor text:**  
Think of science as a giant jigsaw puzzle. Each discovery adds a piece. Sometimes new evidence shows that an earlier idea needs to change. As we learn more, we may also discover new questions. The puzzle is a metaphor for learning; it is not a literal picture of how every scientific discovery happens.

**Tanglish tutor text:**  
Science-ai oru periya jigsaw puzzle madhiri nenachukkalaam. Ovvoru pudhu discovery-um puzzle-la oru piece serkkura madhiri. Sila neram pudhu evidence vandhaa, namma pazhaya idea-vai maathanum. Pudhusaa kathukkumbodhu innum pudhu kelvigalum varalaam. Idhu science learning-ai puriya vaikka use panra oru example.

**Inline drawing:** Three connected puzzle pieces labelled “Question”, “Discovery”, “New question”; one loose piece represents something still to learn.
**Narration beat:** Add each piece as the corresponding idea is spoken.
**Quality check:** Clearly a metaphor; do not suggest knowledge is always a simple linear sequence.

### 3. Science asks what things are made of and how they behave
**Source chunks:** `f03c46cb-1a45-403b-89b1-7a56640e8b08`, `4a6a000d-ae81-4737-8121-f52375ed449c` · **Pages:** 4–5

**English tutor text:**  
Look at the objects around us: paper, a metal key, a plastic ruler, a rubber eraser, a magnet, clothes and a cup. We can ask what each object is made of, whether different materials have different properties, and how materials can be separated. We can also investigate water changing to ice when cooled and to steam when heated, and ask how we measure hot and cold. These questions lead to topics explored later in the book.

**Tanglish tutor text:**  
Namma pakkathula paper, metal key, plastic ruler, rubber eraser, magnet, clothes, cup nu pala porutkal irukku. Ovvoru porulum edhanaala seyyappattadhu, materials-oda properties enna, materials-ai eppadi separate pannalaam nu kelvi kekkalaam. Water-ai cool panninaa ice aagum; heat panninaa steam aagum. Hot-aa cold-aa irukku-nu eppadi measure pannradhu-nu kooda investigate pannalaam. Indha kelvigal book-la varra pala science topics-kku introduction.

**Inline drawing:** A row of the source-listed objects, labelled by object name only; beside it, a small water → ice / water → steam concept with “cool” and “heat” arrows. Do not assign unverified cup/clothing materials.
**Narration beat:** Highlight each object while named, then switch the same inline drawing area to the water-state sketch as narration moves to water.
**Quality check:** Keep the objects and water concept as two labelled sub-scenes in the same content flow; do not overcrowd one tiny diagram.

### 4. Test a guess instead of treating it as a fact
**Source chunks:** `4a6a000d-ae81-4737-8121-f52375ed449c`, `8caf1fd6-b2f3-436b-a7b9-f37c99e44e99`, `0988f81b-21f7-4e16-9f5b-8224f72a1767` · **Pages:** 5–6

**English tutor text:**  
Imagine that your pen stops writing. First ask, “Why did it stop?” One possible guess is that the ink has run out. Open the pen and check the refill. If it is empty, the evidence supports your guess. If ink remains, that guess is not supported and you need to think of another possible reason, such as dried ink, and test it. A guess is not confirmed until you check it.

**Tanglish tutor text:**  
Un pen write pannaama ninnuduchu-nu nenachukko. Mudhal-la, “Yen pen ninnuduchu?” nu kelvi kelu. Ink mudinjirukkum-nu oru guess pannalaam. Pen-ai open panni refill-ai check pannu. Refill empty-aa irundhaa, andha evidence un guess-ai support pannum. Ink irundhaa, andha guess correct-nu solla mudiyadhu; ink dry aayirukkalaam-nu innoru possibility-ai test pannanum. Check pannaama guess-ai fact-nu sollakoodadhu.

**Inline drawing:** Pen → question → “ink empty?” as a tentative guess → inspect refill → two branches: “empty: supported” and “ink remains: test another possibility”.
**Narration beat:** Reveal the test and branch only when those words are narrated.
**Quality check:** Do not imply that an empty refill proves every possible cause; it only supports this particular guess.

### 5. The scientific method is a sequence of investigation steps
**Source chunks:** `0988f81b-21f7-4e16-9f5b-8224f72a1767`, `35d81817-d498-4c4c-9d46-d826259ece45`, `d5075a26-dc9d-457b-aab7-de1059240635` · **Pages:** 6–7

**English tutor text:**  
A useful investigation often follows these steps: observe something, ask a question, suggest a possible answer, test it using an experiment or more observations, and analyse the results. If the results do not support the guess, revise it and investigate again. People use this kind of thinking in everyday life, not only in laboratories.

**Tanglish tutor text:**  
Oru investigation-la indha steps useful-aa irukkum: mudhal-la observe pannu; appuram question kelu; oru possible answer-ai guess pannu; experiment illa innum observations moolama test pannu; kadaisila results-ai analyse pannu. Results guess-ai support pannala-na, guess-ai revise panni marubadiyum investigate pannu. Indha thinking laboratory-la mattum illa; daily life-layum use pannalaam.

**Inline drawing:** Five connected steps: Observe → Ask → Possible explanation → Test → Check results. A return arrow from “check results” to “possible explanation” shows that a guess may need revision.
**Narration beat:** Highlight one step at a time.
**Quality check:** Remove all page numbers/timestamps from text. Do not let OCR like “10/18” trigger a fraction visual.

### 6. Everyday problems can use scientific thinking; learning can be collaborative
**Source chunks:** `d5075a26-dc9d-457b-aab7-de1059240635`, `14256fbc-cb64-43bb-8051-56ad2b0a53fe`, `23e47207-f97d-475c-8654-93de2a43a73b` · **Page:** 7

**English tutor text:**  
The textbook gives everyday examples: finding out why dal spills from a cooker, why a bicycle tyre is flat, or why a bulb does not work. Each starts with a question and possible causes that can be checked. Science is also collaborative: people share observations and help one another investigate. We will not answer every question in Grade 6, and that is okay; learning continues over time.

**Tanglish tutor text:**  
Dal cooker-la yen pongi spill aagudhu, cycle tyre yen flat aagudhu, bulb yen work aagala nu daily life-la kelvi kekkalaam. Ovvoru problem-kum possible reason irukkum; adhai check panni evidence paakkanum. Science-la people observations-ai share panni, orutharukku oruthar help panni investigate pannuvaanga. Grade 6-la ella kelvikkum answer kidaikkaama irukkalaam; adhu parava illa. Learning thodarndhu nadakkum.

**Inline drawing:** Three small scenes—cooker, bicycle tyre, bulb/switch—with a shared “question → check” path; two learners sharing observations alongside the path.
**Narration beat:** Highlight each real-life example, then reveal collaboration.
**Quality check:** Do not invent the cause or solution for any example. Show them as questions to investigate.

### 7. Keep exploring and asking “why?”
**Source chunks:** `14256fbc-cb64-43bb-8051-56ad2b0a53fe`, `23e47207-f97d-475c-8654-93de2a43a73b`, `012dbf53-fad3-4312-96fd-1386c8535ba0` · **Pages:** 7–8

**English tutor text:**  
Science is a continuing journey of joyful exploration. Notice something, ask why, and think about how you could look for an answer. You can write down a question and discuss it with friends. You do not need to know everything now; keep observing and learning.

**Tanglish tutor text:**  
Science oru thodarndhu pogura exciting exploration journey. Edhaavadhu gavanichu, “Yen?” nu kelvi kelu; adhukku answer eppadi thedalaam-nu yosichu paaru. Un question-ai ezhuthi vechu friends-kooda discuss pannalaam. Ippo ella vishayathaiyum therinjirukkanum-nu avasiyam illa. Observe pannittu, pudhusa kathukkitte iru.

**Inline drawing:** A child’s observation notebook with a question mark, two learners discussing, and an open path labelled “Keep exploring”. No fake scientific answer.
**Narration beat:** Sketch notebook → question → discussion as the lesson closes.
**Quality check:** Keep it a motivating close, not a new scientific claim.
