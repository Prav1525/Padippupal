# PadippuPal Class 6 Maths Pilot: Lines and Angles

Curriculum source: NCERT Ganita Prakash, Grade 6 Mathematics, English medium, Reprint 2026-27, Chapter 2, Lines and Angles (stored in PadippuPal as fegp102.pdf).

## Outcomes
The learner identifies points, line segments, lines and rays; identifies the vertex and arms of an angle; compares angles by their opening; recognises the angle categories taught in the chapter; and practises measuring/drawing angles where covered in the chapter.

## Lesson sequence
1. Discover geometry in real objects: pencil tip, straight edge, torch beam, room corner.
2. Point: tap to place and label points; explain that a point marks a location.
3. Line segment: drag endpoints A and B; compare routes and identify endpoints.
4. Line: animate extension in both directions.
5. Ray: keep one endpoint fixed and show extension in one direction.
6. Build an angle: two rays share a vertex; highlight vertex and arms.
7. Compare angles: rotate one arm and compare openings; change arm length separately to show that length does not determine angle size.
8. Rotating arms: predict wider/narrower, then rotate and observe.
9. Special angle types: sort examples using the categories and definitions in the exact textbook chapter.
10. Measure angles: estimate first, then practise correct protractor alignment if included in the chapter.
11. Draw angles: place vertex, align baseline, mark and draw second arm.
12. Mixed practice and teach-back: new examples, hints, retry, recap and mastery check.

## Teaching loop
Connect to a familiar example; show one small concept; let the child manipulate the visual; ask one question; give a hint for mistakes; ask the child to try again; then apply the concept to a new example. Keep narration in short segments and sync each segment to a visual event.

## Memory
Persist locally on-device: selected chapter, lesson ID, current step, completed steps, recent answers/mistakes, mastery status, language and last active local date. When the student returns on the same day, restore the state and offer to continue or review. Do not restart automatically. Cross-device memory is a later phase requiring an explicit privacy design.

## Rendering and voice
Separate spoken text from mathematical display notation. Read ∠ABC aloud as “angle ABC” and 30° as “thirty degrees”; never expose raw LaTeX delimiters or dollar signs. Provide replay/pause and an alternate explanation. Keep source filenames, retrieval diagnostics and API errors out of the student-facing interface.

## Acceptance tests
- State survives tutor navigation and app restart.
- Dragging an angle arm visibly changes the angle in the expected direction.
- Comparison depends on opening, not arm length.
- Protractor guidance and readings are correct.
- Speech and visual events stay aligned and can be replayed.
- Wrong answers trigger a hint and retry before revealing the answer.
- No raw maths markup or internal filenames appear.
- Exact terminology, angle categories and exercise order are checked against the stored textbook before release.

This is a syllabus-aligned design specification, not a claim that the app implementation is complete.
